Part 02 · Proposed collection

Two complementary data streams

Our collection includes complete rural AV runs, comparable to existing data portals, plus focused records of the moments when automation degrades or requires driver takeover.

EVENT

takeover-centered records

Each record preserves a synchronized window before, during and after system degradation, disengagement or driver intervention.

Primary outcomeTakeover event
  1. 01
    Detect system degradation

    Track perception quality, localization, planning behavior and control stability before intervention.

  2. 02
    Mark the takeover

    Align failure, warning, driver response and manual-control timestamps.

  3. 03
    Preserve the rural scene

    Retain road geometry, markings, surface condition, traffic, weather and long-tail objects.

  4. 04
    Measure recovery

    Record the remaining safety margin and how the vehicle stabilizes after the driver responds.

Evaluation framework

What made the driver take over?

A takeover timestamp tells us when the driver intervened. The metrics below help explain why—whether the vehicle was running out of road space, approaching another vehicle, or losing system stability.

No single metric determines safety.

We use several signals together to find events that need review. The limits shown here are starting points from literature or simulation, not validated limits for our vehicle.

We measure the remaining margin and recovery.

Question this metric answers

Is there enough time to keep the vehicle inside its lane?

TLC

TLC estimates how long it would take the vehicle to reach a lane boundary if its current motion continued without corrective steering.

How to read it

Smaller TLC = less time to react

If the driver reacts late, little time may remain to prevent a lane departure.

Candidate screening rule

TLCremaining < 0.5 s

Flag the event for closer review; calibrate with simulator and field data.

TLCremaining = TLCat failure − driver reaction time

Run-level data package

What each rural run records

Every repeated run links what the vehicle sensed, where it was, what the automated driving system planned, and how the vehicle responded.

01

Perception & scene

Camera, LiDAR, radar, detected objects, lane boundaries and drivable space.

02

Localization & trajectory

GNSS/IMU, pose, route, planned trajectory and realized vehicle path.

03

Vehicle & control

CAN data, speed, acceleration, steering, commanded states and measured response.

04

Road, environment & driver

Road type, surface condition, weather, automation status and human/AV driving mode.

Designed for portal-style browsing and reuse, following the data organization examples of ADS for Rural America and Automated Vehicles for All (AVA).
